- Neuron-cluster decomposition and I/O-compute pipelining for memory-constrained edge inference
- FPGA and heterogeneous-memory mapping flows for compressed LLMs
- Energy-aware workload modeling (token-distribution binning, batch-size variation) as a first-class optimization axis
- Interception-centric serving for augmented/tool-using LLM workflows

**Open problems:**
- Synchronized partial softmax updates and flat-GEMM underutilization still impose measurable overheads on GPU inference
- Static dataflows suffer performance loss across differing GEMM shapes and hardware configurations
- Per-token dynamic computation conflicts with batched throughput and synchronization at the system level
- Dequantization overhead and memory bandwidth remain bottlenecks on unified-memory consumer silicon
- Quantization to lower bit precision does not uniformly speed up inference across hardware platforms
- KV cache grows linearly with sequence length, constraining long-context inference memory
- Recomputation of already-computed contexts when LLMs interact with external tools wastes forwarding time
- FLOP-based or theoretical-utilization energy estimates misrepresent real-world consumption; optimization effectiveness is workload-dependent
- Embedding-level structural transformations (e.g., Structured Embedding Projection) introduce dataset-dependent latency/memory trade-offs

**Limitations:**
- All analysis is based on titles and abstracts only; no full texts were read, so quantitative results are as reported by authors and not independently verified.
- Most sources here are arXiv preprints (e.g., FlashDecoding++, PowerInfer-2, CORM, InferCept, the early-exit studies, the Apple Silicon profiling and energy studies) and are not peer reviewed.
- The sample is small (16 papers) and skewed toward GPU/edge hardware acceleration and energy measurement; retrieval may not cover the full breadth of inference work.
- Reported speedups use different models, baselines and hardware, so cross-paper numbers are not directly comparable and are not aggregated here.
- One item (LLM in a flash) has only bibliographic metadata and no usable abstract, so it supports no finding.
- Abstracts do not establish commercial traction, deployment scale or product readiness, and nothing here should be read as investment guidance.

## Gaps, limitations and errors
- Missing data: No direct evidence of customers, contracts or deployments for inference-specific products (customer adoption).
- Missing data: No company-level revenue or margin split isolating LLM inference from other AI or memory demand.
- Missing data: No production-scale, peer-reviewed replication of the reported efficiency gains; most evidence is single preprints seen as abstracts only.
- Missing data: No market findings on GPU leaders or cloud providers' inference services, which could shift the competitive picture.
- Missing data: No verified data on DRAM/HBM supply and demand; the tightness claims come from social posts relaying analyst outlooks.
- Missing data: No explanation of the recent sharp price declines in the related stocks.
- Missing data: Scout financials for Ceva appear dated (fiscal 2017), and several patent assignees have no SEC financials.
- Limitation: Research and patent counts are keyword-based, not normalised for overall publishing growth, and recent patent years are undercounted because of publication delay.
- Limitation: Most science and risk findings rest on abstracts and preprints, so results are best-case and reproducibility is uncertain.
- Limitation: Market evidence comes from short filing excerpts. Company descriptions of technology are not proof of adoption or profitability.
- Limitation: Social-media findings are unverified signals and are used only as context.
- Limitation: Price and timing findings cover about 100 trading days and are risk context only, not a signal.
- Limitation: The next-runner scores are heuristics, not predictions.
- Limitation: This is a research assessment, not investment advice, and it makes no price, return or timing forecasts.
- Chief review gap: Speculative decoding (a major inference acceleration technique) has no dedicated finding; it is only mentioned in passing as a variant of early-exit in S5
- Chief review gap: KV cache management and serving-system optimizations (e.g., PagedAttention, continuous batching, vLLM-class systems) are absent from science findings and appear only in patent signals (P2)
- Chief review gap: Memory-bandwidth-bound analysis of the decode phase (arithmetic intensity, roofline) is referenced in passing (S8, S12) but no finding provides a dedicated quantitative treatment
- Chief review gap: HBM supply-tightness evidence (X1, X5) rests entirely on unverified social signals; no filing or paper in the set independently confirms the supply-demand projection
- Chief review gap: Several key findings carry low confidence (S5, S9, S13, R2–R4, R7, R8), weakening the evidentiary base for edge and FPGA claims
- Chief review gap: Speculative decoding, a major inference acceleration technique, receives only a passing mention in S4 (Dynamic Self-Speculative Decoding) with no dedicated finding on the technique, its variants, or its practical impact.
- Chief review gap: The fundamental memory-bandwidth-bound nature of the decode phase (as distinct from prefill) is not explicitly analyzed in any finding; S2 covers KV-cache size but not the bandwidth-bound characterization that drives much of the hardware design.
- Chief review gap: Quantization methods (e.g., GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant) and their accuracy trade-offs are only tangentially addressed via S7 (hardware-specific profiling) and R6 (edge limitations); no finding covers the method-level landscape or systematic accuracy degradation.
- Chief review gap: No finding addresses inference tail latency (p99/p999) or cost-per-token economics, both of which were planned risk queries.
- Chief review gap: Several findings are AI interpretations at low confidence (S5, S7, S9, R3, R4, R7, R8), meaning the risk picture rests partly on inference rather than direct measurement.
