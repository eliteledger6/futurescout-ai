**Emerging directions:**
- agentic, self-correcting RAG control loops with explicit evidence-gap auditing
- graph and subgraph retrieval over networked documents
- multi-agent orchestration across heterogeneous datastores
- reference-free automated RAG evaluation metric suites
- domain-specialised RAG benchmarks (medicine, religious studies)
- retrieval-layer privacy and membership-inference attacks plus trust frameworks

**Open problems:**
- Hallucination and lack of faithfulness persist even with retrieval, as stated across several abstracts.
- Complex multi-hop queries requiring synthesis from disparate sources remain a weak point of iterative and adaptive RAG.
- No agreed best practice for combining corpora, retrievers and backbone LLMs; component choice is described as fragmented with a lack of guidelines.
- Noise propagation and failure to gather comprehensive context when retrieval goes wrong or evidence gaps are misjudged.
- Retrieved documents in the model context create leakage and membership-inference exposure that parameter-only defences do not cover.
- Evaluation difficulty: judging retrieval relevance, faithfulness and generation quality at once, ideally without ground-truth annotations.
- Smaller models are reported to lag larger ones on query semantics and contextually grounded answers.
- Long-context position sensitivity, including lost-in-the-middle effects, inside RAG pipelines.

**Limitations:**
- All findings rest on titles, metadata and abstracts only; no full paper was read, so methods, baselines and error bars cannot be checked.
- Two items in the set are unusable as evidence: one conference paper's abstract contains only an author list (src_34b5ac3bb8) and one survey has no abstract at all (src_668d84068e).
- Several key items are arXiv preprints and are not peer reviewed; the corrective, FAIR-RAG, privacy, multi-agent and survey results are therefore unrefereed.
- Many performance numbers quoted here are author-reported on the authors' own benchmarks and are not independently reproduced within this analysis.
- Some abstracts are truncated mid-sentence, so reported metrics and conclusions may be incomplete.
- The sample is small and heavily skewed toward one dominant technique (agentic/iterative RAG); graph RAG, privacy and multi-agent evidence each rest on a single paper.
- No paper in this set is evidence of commercial adoption, and nothing here supports any investment conclusion.
