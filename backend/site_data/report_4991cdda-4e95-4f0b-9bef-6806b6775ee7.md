## Evidence: risks and contrarian findings
Status: ok. Risk search queries: ['base editor off-target effects', 'base editor bystander mutations', 'base editor delivery limitations', 'base editor editing efficiency', 'base editor immunogenicity', 'base editor regulatory concerns']

- **[R1]** *(verified_fact, confidence medium)* The most efficient adenine base editor, ABE8e, showed prevalent genome-wide off-target SNVs in embryo-injection assays (~30-fold above spontaneous SNVs), whereas its parent ABE7.10 did not. This suggests a trade-off between potency and genomic fidelity in the editors most used for therapeutic development.
  - source: [Therapeutic adenine base editor with minimized off-target effects](https://doi.org/10.1093/procel/pwag006) (OpenAlex, 2026-02-01, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "the rate of genome-wide single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) in ABE8e-edited cells was ∼30-fold higher than that of spontaneous SNVs in control cells"
  - reasoning: Single abstract. The assay is two-cell mouse embryo injection (GOTI), so the result may not transfer to clinical delivery (transient expression in human somatic cells). The fix (ABE8eY149V) is shown only in mice and cells, and no clinical safety data appear in the abstract.
- **[R2]** *(verified_fact, confidence medium)* Bystander editing is a functional, not just theoretical, failure mode. In a Leber congenital amaurosis mouse model, ABE8e bystander edits created a missense mutation (L43P) that disrupted RPE65 and prevented visual restoration, even though RPE65 was expressed.
  - source: [Bystander editing by adenine base editors impairs vision restoration in a mouse model of Leber congenital amaurosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omtm.2025.101461) (OpenAlex, 2025-04-03, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "ABE8e, generated substantial bystander editing, resulting in missense mutations despite RPE65 expression, preventing restoration of visual function."
  - reasoning: One mouse-model study. The mechanistic explanation relies partly on AlphaFold-based scanning and molecular dynamics, which are computational predictions. It shows that the highest-activity editor may be the wrong choice for some target sequences.
- **[R3]** *(verified_fact, confidence medium)* Long-term in vivo safety of cytosine base editors is a documented concern. Sustained BE3 expression in transgenic mice produced de novo germline mutations, transcriptome-wide RNA SNVs correlated with expression level, and abnormal phenotypes such as obesity and developmental delay. High-fidelity YE1-BE3-FNLS still induced RNA SNVs.
  - source: [Cytosine base editors induce off-target mutations and adverse phenotypic effects in transgenic mice](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37508-7) (OpenAlex, 2023-03-30, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "BE3 and YE1-BE3-FNLS induce transcriptome-wide SNVs, and the numbers of RNA SNVs are positively correlated with CBE expression levels across various tissues."
  - reasoning: The study used permanent transgenic overexpression, which is much higher exposure than transient therapeutic delivery. The phenotypes may therefore overstate the clinical risk. It does show that a high-fidelity variant is not automatically clean. Evidence comes from one study of ~400 mice.
- **[R4]** *(ai_interpretation, confidence low)* Reducing bystander editing by narrowing the activity window or by directed evolution appears to be context dependent. This implies a universal precise editor is unlikely, and per-target (personalized) guide/enzyme optimization may be needed, which adds development cost and regulatory burden.
  - source: [Engineered base editors with reduced bystander editing through directed evolution](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-025-02937-w) (OpenAlex, 2025-12-18, abstract_only)
  - source: [A streamlined base editor engineering strategy to reduce bystander editing](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63609-6) (OpenAlex, 2025-08-30, abstract_only)
  - source: [Bystander editing by adenine base editors impairs vision restoration in a mouse model of Leber congenital amaurosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omtm.2025.101461) (OpenAlex, 2025-04-03, abstract_only)

## Gaps, limitations and errors
- Missing data: Human clinical efficacy, durability and safety data for in vivo base editors (only unverified social posts)
- Missing data: Verified papers behind the X2 (ANGPTL3 primate) and X3 (AATD liver biopsy) claims
- Missing data: Manufacturing, delivery-vehicle and supply-chain evidence
- Missing data: Customer, payer and regulatory-approval evidence
- Missing data: Price and timing risk findings (none provided)
- Missing data: Company evidence is limited to a few filers; many assignees and private companies have no financials
- Missing data: Full text of papers: several cited records are abstract-only or metadata-only
- Limitation: Research counts are not normalised for general publishing growth and include some non-biological uses of 'editing'.
- Limitation: Patent counts are undercounted in recent years because of publication delay, and applications are claims rather than granted rights.
- Limitation: Social posts are unverified signals; headline and press-release evidence is company-authored.
- Limitation: This is a research assessment for a 1-5 year view, not a recommendation, price target or return forecast.
- Chief review gap: 50% of retrieved papers (6 of 12) are off-topic (NLP knowledge-base editing, image/video editing, topological edit distance), diluting the science evidence base
- Chief review gap: No specific finding addresses immunogenicity of base editors (anti-Cas9 or anti-deaminase immune responses in humans), despite it being a planned query
- Chief review gap: Delivery evidence is limited to AAV9 (SMN mouse model); LNP-mediated delivery, which is the clinically relevant route for Beam and Verve, lacks dedicated science findings on efficiency, tropism, or limitations
- Chief review gap: Editing efficiency data is from mouse models and cell lines; no finding reports efficiency in human primary cells or humanized models beyond the CAR T application
- Chief review gap: Science findings are thin on editor variant comparison (ABE7.10 vs ABE8e vs BE4max vs TadA-derived editors) and PAM constraints, which matter for target selection in therapeutic design
