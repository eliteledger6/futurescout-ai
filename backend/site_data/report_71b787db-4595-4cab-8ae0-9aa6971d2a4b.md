- Non-uniqueness of counterfactual safe-default mappings in runtime risk pricing; lack of longitudinal/empirical validation under real API evolution

## Gaps, limitations and errors
- Missing data: Agent-specific revenue, customer counts and usage from vendors (only product descriptions in filings)
- Missing data: Independent evidence of customer adoption and measured return on investment
- Missing data: Compute, chip and other supply-chain evidence for agent workloads beyond one Cisco statement and unverified social posts
- Missing data: Empirical validation of agent governance, authorization and visibility proposals
- Missing data: Experimentally validated reliability standards for agents and multi-agent systems
- Missing data: Price and timing data for Salesforce, Atlassian, BILL and other named vendors
- Missing data: Content of the 'Governing AI Agents' item (only metadata was available)
- Missing data: Verification of X-sourced claims (Anthropic models, NVIDIA, Google TPUs, Apple risk disclosure, Spines funding)
- Limitation: Research counts are keyword-based, not normalised for overall publishing growth, and include many preprints and abstract-only analyses.
- Limitation: Patent counts are applications (claims, not proof) and recent years are undercounted by publication delay.
- Limitation: Market evidence is company self-description in filings; headline-based and social-media items are unverified and given little weight.
- Limitation: Price and timing metrics cover about 100 trading days for six stocks and are risk context only, not signals.
- Limitation: Company Scout scores are heuristics, not predictions; smaller companies carry more risk and less disclosure.
- Limitation: This is a research assessment, not investment advice, and contains no price targets or buy/sell/hold recommendations.
- Chief review gap: No quantitative data on inference cost or latency at production scale; cost is mentioned qualitatively (S1) but no specific figures on token consumption or cost-per-task for deployed agents.
- Chief review gap: Multi-agent coordination reliability is covered only at the conceptual level (R7); no empirical failure-rate data as agent count scales.
- Chief review gap: Agent memory and persistence reliability is identified as a defining feature of Agentic AI (S10) but no finding addresses how memory systems perform or degrade over time.
- Chief review gap: One retrieved paper (Governing AI Agents, S13) could not be assessed due to metadata-only availability.
- Chief review gap: X findings are all low-confidence unverified claims and should not be treated as evidence in the report.
