- **[R1]** *(verified_fact, confidence low)* Multi-step agentic RAG introduces a failure mode in which an early retrieval or reasoning error propagates and grows across later steps. The authors say existing output-level detectors systematically miss it.
  - source: [Cascading Hallucination in Agentic RAG: The CHARM Framework for Detection and Mitigation](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.04435) (OpenAlex, 2026-06-03, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "cascading hallucination, where errors introduced at early pipeline stages propagate and amplify across successive reasoning steps, producing confident but factually incorrect final outputs."
  - reasoning: This rests on one arXiv preprint, which is not peer reviewed. The proposed CHARM fix is evaluated by its own authors, partly on a custom adversarial dataset. The reported figures (89.4% detection, 215 ms overhead per stage) also show the mitigation adds latency at every stage. Independent replication is a gap.
- **[R2]** *(verified_fact, confidence low)* The external knowledge base is an attack surface. Dense-retrieval RAG can be poisoned with adversarial passages, and newer attacks are designed to be stealthier and more persistent.
  - source: [Entity-Anchored Multi-Document Poisoning Attacks on RAG Systems](https://doi.org/10.1109/eitce70137.2026.11634562) (OpenAlex, 2026-06-01, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "the heavy reliance on external knowledge bases introduces severe data poisoning vulnerabilities"
  - reasoning: This is one conference abstract describing an attack method. It gives no information on real-world prevalence or on how well defenses work. Attack success rates are reported only for the authors' own setups.
- **[R3]** *(ai_interpretation, confidence medium)* Retrieval does not remove hallucination. Models can still distort or misread the retrieved sources, and a Nature paper argues the cause is structural: next-word training and accuracy-based benchmarks reward guessing over admitting uncertainty.
  - source: [RAG-HAT: A Hallucination-Aware Tuning Pipeline for LLM in Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-industry.113) (OpenAlex, 2024-01-01, abstract_only)

## Gaps, limitations and errors
- Missing data: Revenue, customer counts or usage figures attributable to RAG
- Missing data: Independent validation of benchmark gains and evaluation metrics
- Missing data: Market share, pricing and competitive positioning data for RAG vendors and cloud platforms
- Missing data: Supply-chain or infrastructure cost evidence specific to RAG
- Missing data: Absolute false-negative rates of hallucination detectors under production conditions
- Missing data: Price and filing data for small RAG-active companies (only large-cap price series are in the timing set)
- Limitation: Research counts are not normalised for overall publishing growth, and phrase matching (including the abbreviation RAG) may include off-topic works.
- Limitation: Many science findings rest on abstracts or single preprints; some records lack abstracts (the active-retrieval and evaluation-survey sources).
- Limitation: Patent counts are undercounted for recent years due to publication delay and applications are claims, not proof.
- Limitation: The large-cap companies in the timing set are only loosely linked to RAG; price metrics are risk context only, not timing guidance.
- Limitation: Social posts are promotional or educational signals, not evidence of adoption.
- Limitation: Company scout scores are heuristics, not predictions of returns; smaller companies carry more risk and less disclosure.
- Limitation: This is a research assessment, not investment advice.
- Chief review gap: No finding directly addresses whether long-context LLMs (1M+ token windows) are displacing or complementing RAG — a key strategic question for the investment thesis; the planned query 'long context vs retrieval' produced no dedicated finding
