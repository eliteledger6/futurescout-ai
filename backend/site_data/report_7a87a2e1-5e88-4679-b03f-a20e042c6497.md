- **[R1]** *(verified_fact, confidence medium)* VLA policies that score very well in-distribution can fail when a small change in the scene requires a different action for the same instruction. The paper calls this 'instruction-action binding' failure. Aggregate robustness scores can hide it, and the proposed fix leaves substantial failures (position-swap success rises only from 36% to 59% for pi0.5 on LIBERO-PRO).
  - source: [When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories: Diagnosing and Mitigating Generalization Failures in VLA Models](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.39971) (OpenAlex, 2026-09-30, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "Vision-language-action (VLA) models can exceed 90% success on in-distribution tasks and withstand nuisance changes that preserve the required action, yet fail under counterfactual changes that demand a different action."
  - reasoning: This is a single arXiv preprint (not peer reviewed), based on abstract text only and on simulated benchmarks. The 36%->59% figure is the authors' own mitigation result, so even the fixed model still fails often on this test.
- **[R2]** *(verified_fact, confidence medium)* An open benchmark reports that state-of-the-art VLAs tend to memorize rather than generalize, rely on superficial visual perception, and neglect safety constraints. Rank reversals across difficulty levels suggest that single-number leaderboards can mislead.
  - source: [VLA-Arena: An Open-Source Framework for Benchmarking Vision-Language-Action Models](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2512.22539) (OpenAlex, 2025-12-27, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "Our extensive evaluation of state-of-the-art VLAs reveals critical limitations: memorization over generalization, superficial visual perception, and a neglect of safety constraints."
  - reasoning: This is an arXiv preprint, abstract only. Results come from a simulated benchmark designed by the authors, and fine-tuning is restricted to the easiest level. That setup may exaggerate failures relative to real deployments, so the findings need replication on other benchmarks.
- **[R3]** *(verified_fact, confidence medium)* Compositional generalization is a documented weak point. VLAs struggle with skill combinations absent from fine-tuning data even when each skill was demonstrated, and a vision shortcut (observations standing in for the instruction) is one proposed cause. This is consistent with the instruction-keyed failures reported in other preprints.
  - source: [Same Scene, Different Task: Skill Alignment for Compositional Generalization in VLAs](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2610.00524) (OpenAlex, 2026-09-30, abstract_only)
  - source: [When Instructions Retrieve Trajectories: Diagnosing and Mitigating Generalization Failures in VLA Models](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.39971) (OpenAlex, 2026-09-30, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "often struggle to generalize to skill combinations absent from their fine-tuning demonstrations, even when every constituent skill has been demonstrated."
  - reasoning: Two independent preprints (not peer reviewed) point to related shortcut learning from narrow demonstration data. Both proposed fixes (CRAFT, ECT) rely on extra counterfactual data or supervision, which implies added data cost. Abstracts only.
- **[R4]** *(verified_fact, confidence low)* Frozen VLAs may contain good behaviors that they do not reliably select.   - source: [Look Before You Leap: Distilling Tree Search into Action Evaluation for Frozen VLA Models](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.03751) (OpenAlex, 2026-07-04, abstract_only)
  - excerpt: "overall success rates rising from 33% at pass@1 to 92% at pass@32"
  - reasoning: One arXiv preprint, abstract truncated. The gap between pass@1 and pass@k shows an unreliable default policy. The simulator dependency during training of the evaluator is a transfer risk to real-world settings that the abstract does not address.
